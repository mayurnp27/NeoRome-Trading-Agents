Why the estimated advantage failed

The model overestimated the win rates of selected A and B opportunities. Across every qualifying one-contract signal, A predicted 82.59% wins and achieved 79.29%; B predicted 58.64% and achieved 53.10%. Those errors were larger than the estimated trading advantages. Both forecasts also had slightly worse Brier scores than the Kalshi midpoint baseline on the chronological validation slice and the final period.

C rejected 1,602 of its 1,605 original strong-lead signals for insufficient estimated edge. The three accepted opportunities had very small estimated advantages, averaging less than 0.1¢ per contract. They were lower-probability, cheaper contracts; the high-probability contracts generally cost too much after fees. Their resulting 33.33% win rate does not contradict the earlier 93.15% rate across all strong-lead signals.

How probabilities were estimated

Two fixed ridge-logistic models predict whether the original +5/+10 signal side wins. Inputs are the chosen-side Kalshi midpoint at delayed entry, a Gaussian transform of the prior normalized BTC lead, and trade direction. Coefficients shrink toward a midpoint-only prediction. A separate logistic intercept/slope calibrates probabilities. Feature choices, ridge penalty 10 and all edge thresholds were fixed before the cash results were inspected; no winner was chosen afterward.

Training: August 18–31, with 1,326 valid +5-minute and 1,325 valid +10-minute valid rows; calibration: September 2–6, 471 rows each. Only outcomes settled before the respective September 1 and 7 cutoffs are used. September 1, 7 and 12 market-start days are embargoed. Validation is assigned by market start; a late September 11 outcome may settle during September 12, but validation never influences fitting or selection. Models and row hashes were saved before cash replay.

The original longer July archive plan was revised only because public archived requests hit repeated rate limits, before any model fit or new P&L inspection. It is retained as a superseded plan. The revised complete pre-test quote window contains 2,367 markets retrieved in 119 public batches; partial July samples are excluded entirely. The short training and calibration windows are a material limitation.

Accounting, validation and limits

YES uses the historical ask; NO uses one minus the YES bid. The largest whole-contract order whose premium plus fees fits 25% of available cash is sized first, then the estimated edge gate is applied. Cash is released only at official settlement. There is at most one open position per account, no leverage, resets or shared funds. The audited standard quadratic taker fee and direct-member rounding are inherited from the prior replay; cent rounding is separately tested.

Six focused tests passed. The independent audit reconstructed all 49,800 decisions, 3,247 trades and 6,494 cash events. It checked source quotes/features, every predicted probability, fitted-objective gradients, label cutoffs, no final/validation labels in fitting, exact fee-inclusive sizing/gates, official payouts and cash chronology. Read-only Luna review accepted the methodology with the documented validation-boundary note.

This is an already inspected historical period, not a fresh untouched holdout. Depth and fills are unverified; using the quote as a feature and assuming immediate execution at it does not establish order latency. Final-metadata strike publication and Coinbase/BRTI basis remain unresolved. The results test this specific estimated-probability model, not every possible model or whether genuinely positive expected value can be profitable.

Research decision: the price filter alone does not establish an edge. None of these accounts qualifies for promotion on this evidence. A new experiment would need more pre-period data, demonstrably better probability calibration conditional on price, and prospective quote/depth validation before any profitability claim.
