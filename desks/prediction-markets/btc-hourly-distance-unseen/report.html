Frozen distance filter on unused BTC markets

All four $500 accounts finished profitable in the February–March transfer test, including a 1¢ worse-entry stress. The result remains uncertain: the 25% account suffered a 56.6% drawdown, and the estimated average edge's confidence interval includes losses.

The rule was unchanged: original stochastic/SMA vote plus absolute distance from the fixed Kalshi strike of at least one estimated volatility unit. Signal at minute 15, hypothetical entry at minute 16, 44 minutes before expiry. Accounts hold until official settlement; allocation includes fees.

Data coverage and exclusions

All planned periods were collected without choosing a replacement date based on performance. The older window contains 1,320 possible hourly starts; 1,300 had an eligible listed contract under the frozen selection rule. The 20 missing eligible hours are listed in the prediction lock. One missing Coinbase five-minute candle caused 24 selected hours to fail feature completeness; no interpolation was used. Three additional selected contracts had unusable settlement metadata, all with no qualifying signal.

The filter produced 111 older signals. Exact entry quotes were available for 110. One February 5 signal had no archived minute-16 quote; a full-hour historical request and the batch quote endpoint were also empty. That signal was skipped, not assigned an estimated price. The result is conditional on this quote coverage. All 22 October hours and both qualifying entry quotes were available.

Legacy metadata qualification

All 1,300 archived contracts carry exchange_index 0, while current contracts carry 2. Bounded official event, historical-market and series requests did not resolve that mapping. All selected records use the same KXBTCD family, BRTI final sixty-second average, strict above-strike condition and binary dollar payout. The older calculations are therefore retained as a legacy-market transfer check with an explicit continuity caveat, not presented as proof of current-exchange equivalence or forward profitability.

Exactly what stayed fixed

The two components are the 15-minute stochastic (67,1,1) %D slope and a fresh 45-minute SMA(2,3) cross. The vote is the sign of their sum: a component can act alone, opposing signals cancel, and missing components fail closed. This is not a requirement that both indicators fire.

The filter is abs(spot − strike) / (spot × sigma5m × sqrt(9)) ≥ 1, using the completed five-minute close at minute 15 and sample standard deviation of the preceding 288 complete simple five-minute returns. It is a volatility scale, not a calibrated probability. The nearest strike is selected once at the hour's opening using the preceding completed five-minute close, with ties broken by lower strike then ticker. Selection does not use eventual volume, results or entry prices. Only completed epoch-aligned indicator candles are used.

Actual quotes, reconstructed fees, hypothetical fills

Prices are official historical Kalshi YES asks, or 1 minus YES bid for NO, at the exact entry timestamp. They are not settlement prices, midpoints or fitted probabilities. They support a hypothetical full fill; historical available size, queue position and publication latency were not verified. The four paper accounts are internal simulations, not broker account executions.

Primary older fees use ceil_cent(0.07 × quantity × price × (1 − price)), supported by indexed text of Kalshi's February 5 schedule. The PDF at that URL now serves the July 7 version, so the original February document was not recovered as an immutable historical file. This is an explicit fee-version provenance limitation. Current series history has no fee changes, and no KXBTCD event override was returned. That absence does not itself prove the historical global rounding rule. Verification and reproducibility

Predictions were hashed and frozen before entry-quote collection and profit calculations. New target hours and tickers have zero overlap with the original hourly selection dataset. All 24 account/scenario replays have exact-rational checks of fees, maximum affordable integer size, event cash balances, settlement timing and the 44-minute entry horizon. Source inputs and code are hashed; the independent read-only review is saved separately.

The frozen rule was discovered after extensive earlier indicator testing. An unused historical period reduces direct reuse of those outcomes, but cannot remove all selection uncertainty. These results support keeping the rule as a candidate; they do not establish prospective profitability or justify live deployment.
