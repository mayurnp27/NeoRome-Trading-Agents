Why the long-lookback stochastic can be accurate late. With three-period D smoothing, D(t)−D(t−1) equals [K(t)−K(t−3)]/3. On a 15-minute chart, this compares K now with K 45 minutes earlier. When the longer high/low range is stable, its sign often tracks the move already observed during the hour. That explains why a high late-entry win rate can add little beyond the current-price baseline.

What was searched and how winners were selected

For each target: stochastic lookback 5–100 candles, K smoothing 1–5 and D smoothing 1–5; eight rules and normal/inverted orientations. SMA tests every pair 2≤fast<slow≤200, with state and fresh crossover rules, normal/inverted. Each is tested on 15-, 30-, 45- and 60-minute candles at entries +0, +15, +30 and +45. That is 1,875,264 single-chart variants per target, 3,750,528 total. Degenerate and duplicate variants count as searched; shortlists deduplicate identical training decisions.

Stochastic rules: level around 50, K−D direction, K slope, D slope, fresh K/D crossover, and rising from a previous K at/below 10/20/30 or falling from at/above 90/80/70. The latter are zone events, not necessarily local reversals. “Inverted” means reversing the conventional signal. SMA state means fast above/below slow; crossover fires only on a fresh sign change.

Only complete source candles are allowed. 45-minute candles use fixed UTC epoch boundaries; they do not restart at each market hour. State signals carry from the latest completed candle, while event signals fire only at an exact source-candle close. Thus a 60-minute state cannot gain new information within an hour. Missing input bars remain missing, including the Coinbase gap on May 8; longer affected lookbacks abstain.

Training is April 21–August 6; August 7 is an embargo. Validation is August 8–September 11; September 12 is an embargo. Labels must settle before the respective cutoff. Evaluation is September 13–October 17. For each family/chart/offset, retain the ten best distinct training decision sequences with positive training correlation and at least 200 active signals. Validation chooses among them with at least 50 active signals. The ranking score is Pearson correlation between signed decisions and official outcomes, with zero for abstention and missing excluded. It is coverage-sensitive and is not a probability estimate.

Combinations exhaustively pair the top five training candidates per family/chart/offset: same-family pairs across charts and all stochastic–SMA pairs. Agreement requires equal nonzero directions; voting takes the sign of the sum, allowing one component to abstain. Additional three/four-chart unanimous combinations use each family/chart’s top training candidate. The combination search is exhaustive within these declared shortlists, not over the full cross-product of millions of raw settings. Family winners can select their entry offset using validation only. Settings and prediction arrays are hashed and saved before evaluation.

Combination trial counts: Kalshi 5,792, Polymarket 5,792. Confidence intervals resample whole UTC days 2,000 times. The sample has only 35 evaluation days; rare rules have much less information. A large grid, a short validation period and prior inspection of this calendar range all increase overfitting risk. No strategy is promoted to live trading.

Limits that matter

  • These are two different settlement targets and two different feature feeds; their win rates are not interchangeable.
  • The price/strike baseline is a necessary comparison for late entries: the market often prices in the same information that improves accuracy.
  • Final market metadata are retrospective snapshots. Original strike publication/revisions and later outcome revisions cannot be reconstructed completely.
  • Kalshi uses Coinbase as a feature proxy for its BRTI settlement index; Polymarket uses its stated Binance source. Missing markets and exclusions are listed in the data summaries.
  • The 25% position allocation is the user-specified experiment. Compounding, occasional losses and fee drag can substantially change a small account even when signal accuracy looks favorable.
  • All thresholds, tested ranges and selection decisions are finite. “Best” means validation-selected within this experiment, not the best possible strategy.

An independent audit caught a floating-point equality error in one initial SMA diagnostic. Both entire searches were rerun using exact cent-valued sums, with unchanged rules. First-attempt outputs are retained; this correction does not restore an untouched holdout.
