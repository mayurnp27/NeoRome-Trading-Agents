Explanation of the accepted historical study · 18 October 2026

What we predicted, when we entered, and why the account lost money

A correct prediction and a profitable purchase are different measurements. The combined Kalshi signal was correct 63.70% of the time, but its average one-contract entry cost including fees was 67.27¢ for a contract paying $1 when correct.

1. Three prices with different jobs

PriceWhat it meansExample, hypothetical
Market benchmark / strikeThe price used to decide the winning side at settlement$100,000
BTC price at entryCurrent underlying price; does not replace the market benchmark$100,400
Contract purchase priceWhat we pay for one YES/NO or UP/DOWN contract66¢ per contract, plus fees

If settlement is $100,100, the Above/Up side wins against a $100,000 benchmark even though BTC fell after a $100,400 entry. For Kalshi KXBTCD, settlement is its final 60-second BRTI average compared with the listed strike. For Polymarket hourly Up/Down, settlement compares Binance hourly close with hourly open; equality counts as Up.

2. The actual early-entry schedule

Within the hourly marketAction
Minute 0Identify the exact contract and keep its benchmark fixed.
Minute 15Calculate signals using only completed candles; no future minute 30 data.
Minute 16Modeled fill one minute after the decision, leaving 44 minutes to expiry.
Minute 30Latest permitted entry under the user’s constraint.
Minute 60Market expiry and settlement benchmark measurement. Cash arrives at the recorded settlement timestamp, which can be later.

Earlier 75–77% results used a signal calculated from candles finishing at minute 30. A delayed fill would occur at minute 31 and violate the new deadline. The compliant decision 29 version must use older completed candles: the newest 15-minute candle ended at 15 and the newest 30-minute candle ended at 0. Forming-candle indicators were not tested.

3. Exactly how the two indicators vote

ComponentKalshi selected settingsPolymarket selected settingsDirectional vote
Stochastic15 m chart;67 lookback, K=1, D=115 m chart;62 lookback, K=1, D=1Rising D → +1; falling D → −1; flat →0.
SMA crossover45 m chart;SMA 2 crossing SMA 3SameFresh upward cross →+1; downward cross →−1; no fresh cross →0.

Stochastic measures the close’s position inside its lookback high–low range. With K/D smoothing set to 1, K and D are identical. This selected rule uses the line’s slope, not an overbought/oversold threshold. SMA uses 2 and 3 candles, not calendar days. The 45-minute candles follow fixed UTC boundaries; a new candle does not close at minute 15 in every hour.

This is a voting strategy, not a requirement that both indicators independently confirm every trade. When the SMA has no fresh crossover, the stochastic can supply the only active vote.

The minute 16 strategy also failed to improve on a simple price comparison. One exact-price tie makes Kalshi’s comparison denominator 773 instead of 774; the paired difference is−1.94 percentage points.

Prices and fees varied by signal. The 67.271¢ figure is their average, not a fixed price on every trade. This calculation uses one equal-sized contract per signal; it does not describe the variable-size $500 account. For a hypothetical contract costing 67¢ all-in, a win earns 33¢ while a loss loses 67¢. Counting wins alone hides that asymmetry.

Different trades bought different numbers of contracts, so 62.90% winning trades does not mean 62.90% of purchased contracts won. The contract-weighted win rate was 53.53%. Sizing at 25% of current cash also compounds the path: a losing trade removes roughly a quarter of the then-current balance, and the next allocation is based on the reduced balance. Recoveries occurred—the balance rose from $25.89 after 50 trades to $144.02 after 100—but the account later depleted.

Before fees, payouts minus premiums already lost $409.74. Fees added $89.6626. The loss was not caused by fees alone. The single-contract calculation also lost money, so the problem is not solely the 25% allocation.

8. What this does and does not establish

This tests prediction of each market’s official benchmark outcome, with an entry deadline and completed candles. It does not forecast BTC’s exact closing dollar price. It does not establish profitable incremental value over the simple current-price comparison.

Evaluation:September 13–October 17,2026, a 35-day period already inspected in earlier research. These are exploratory results. Daily-block 95% interval for the equal-contract mean net runs approximately−7.05¢ to+0.11¢; the observed loss is not proof of permanently negative expected value. Quotes model full fills without historical depth/queue proof. No executable Polymarket ask history was established, so its profit is unverified.

This explanation adds no new strategy selection, data collection, or account replay. It derives directly from accepted outputs and ledgers. All original research files remain unchanged.
