What this can and cannot support

  • Official Kalshi yes/no results are the target. Kalshi uses averages of CF Benchmarks BRTI around the contract boundaries, not Coinbase close-to-close prices.
  • Only fully closed candles are used. Coinbase history is a retrospective data source; availability delay at the exact boundary and vendor revisions are not modeled. Events occur only at their chart's close boundary.
  • Lookback 5–60; K and D SMA smoothing 1–5; 5/15/30-minute charts; eight rules in both orientations. Combinations use ten distinct training-selected signals per timeframe, all pairs and triples, with mean and unanimous rules.
  • Final confidence intervals use 2,000 daily-block resamples. They do not remove every selection or regime risk; the three secondary comparisons are not multiplicity-adjusted. The inspected holdout cannot be reused as untouched data.
  • Observed accuracy of 51.87% implies a descriptive average all-in break-even cost of 0.5187 dollars per $1 payout, assuming the same future hit rate and fixed cost. Its lower block bound is 0.4790. Neither is a quote or validated buying limit. Entry asks, fees, depth and latency were not backtested; no P&L claim is made.
  • Existing strategy code, paper accounts, and live trading settings were not changed.

Reproduce and inspect

Run collect.py once (cached public responses), test_search.py, then search.py with NumPy and pandas. selection-lock.json records the pre-holdout choices; trials.csv retains all train/validation trials; holdout-predictions.csv retains the final winner's scores; results.json contains metrics and input hashes.
